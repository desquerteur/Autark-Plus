# Leading social media work

People scroll past almost everything. They stop for a person, a surprise, a tension, something they can use today or
something they recognise in themselves – and they stay when it is specific and reads as written by someone who cares. A
model left alone writes the opposite: correct, balanced, general, and recognisable as AI within a line.

## What works with people
- The first second or line decides: a concrete hook – a fact, a moment, a claim, a question someone really has – not an
  introduction.
- One idea per post, told through one real example, number or story. Specific beats general every time.
- A real voice with a view; who speaks matters as much as what is said.
- Each platform's own form: what works on LinkedIn reads wrong on TikTok; length, picture, video and first line follow
  the place.
- A channel grows through steadiness and replies more than through any single post.

## Where AI writing fails – and I say so in the brief
- Generic words, hype, tidy threes, rows of emojis, "in today's fast-paced world", a moral at the end.
- Safe and bland: I ask for a stance and for what the audience would not expect.
- Invented detail: only real facts, people, numbers and pictures.
- Pictures that decorate instead of telling: generated or real, they carry the content.

## What my brief carries
- The audience as people – who they are, what they already know, what they want – and the one thing they should feel,
  think or do afterwards.
- The voice, shown with real posts that worked and one that did not; an example teaches more than adjectives.
- The material: the true story, facts and pictures it may use, and what it may not say.
- The way: several truly different angles first, not variants of one; then the strongest, sharpened, in the platform's
  form.

## Prompting so the model works differently
A role with taste ("an editor who cuts every line that would bore a friend") and one real reader to write for steer a
model more than a list of rules. I give it room to surprise and judge the result as a reader: would I stop, would I send
it to someone? Before anything is published, someone other than its writer reads it the same way, and what the audience
then does – not my impression – teaches my next brief.
